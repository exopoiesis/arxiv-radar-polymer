# Bending Rigidity of Smectic Filaments

**Authors:** Haotian Long, Shamiah A. Williams, Chinedum O. Osuji, Christopher A. Browne

Many biological and engineered filaments exhibit internal structure or fluidity that alters their elastic behavior. We study the elasticity of microscale filamentous smectic condensates, grown during liquid-liquid crystal phase separation. We use a Fourier analysis of their thermal bend fluctuations to measure their persistence length, which follows a non-classic linear scaling with their cross-sectional area, in contrast to the classic quadratic scaling for elastic filaments. We rationalize this scaling using the Helfrich energy to compute the free energy of bending a filament with internal smectic fluid ordering. Our analysis then provides a direct method to measure the bend modulus K, which quantifies the rigidity of smectic layers; providing the first method to directly measure K for dispersed smectic phases. We quantify the softening of filaments in the presence of shorter alkyl-tail mesogen dopants, decreasing K by up to ~35%. Out results suggest how nanoscale smectic organization and molecular composition determine filament-scale elasticity, informing the control of filament mechanics and self-assembly in biomimetic and engineered materials.
