# Decoupling Entropic and Enthalpic Elasticity in Crosslinked Polymer Nanocomposites

**Authors:** Chiranjit Majhi, Sivasurender Chandran, Krishnacharya Khare

Nanoparticle-induced local interface regions significantly enhance the bulk mechanical behavior of polymer nanocomposites, yet their energetic contribution in crosslinked elastomer networks remains poorly understood. Here, we decouple the entropic and enthalpic contributions to the elasticity of crosslinked poly(dimethylsiloxane) nanocomposites using temperature-dependent thermomechanical measurements. While the measured bulk elastic modulus exhibits a monotonic increase with nanoparticle volume fraction $φ_{\rm n}$, we observe that the effective crosslink density initially increases before saturating at larger $φ_{\rm n}$. These results are independently validated across two nanoparticle systems (silica and titania) and directly confirmed by X-ray scattering experiments on silica-based composites, highlighting the generality of the observation. The trends in effective crosslink density indicate that the elastic response is predominantly entropy-driven at low nanoparticle concentrations, whereas enthalpic contributions become increasingly significant at higher loadings. Assuming a linear superposition of interface and bulk contributions, these results reveal that the effective crosslink density at the particle interface decreases by an order of magnitude below a critical mean interparticle separation, indicating that localized particle proximity and clustering constrain the entropic elasticity at higher loadings.
