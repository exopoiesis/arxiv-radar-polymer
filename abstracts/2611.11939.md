# Topology to Elasticity Mapping in Thermal Glass

**Authors:** Avinash Kumar Jha

Building on our previous work on thermal nonaffine metrics and thermal strain fields, we examine how local steric geometry controls thermally driven rearrangements in an amorphous glass-forming solid. As a structural descriptor, we employ the topological steric "theta-mismatch" order parameter, which quantifies local deviations of bond angles from the steric reference geometry and is used to distinguish low- and high-mismatch particle populations. We then compare their thermal nonaffine response, thermal deviatoric strain, and thermal volumetric strain over a broad temperature range at several state points. High-Delta-theta sites preferentially exhibit larger thermal nonaffine motion and stronger thermal deviatoric strain, particularly in the deeply jammed regime, indicating that geometrically frustrated regions are preferentially associated with localized mechanical distortions. The thermal volumetric response exhibits a distinct mismatch-dependent crossover: low-Delta-theta sites predominantly contribute to the small-strain response, whereas high-Delta-theta sites contribute more strongly to the large-strain tail. The contrast between low- and high-mismatch populations is most pronounced in the deeply jammed regime, remains identifiable through the intermediate viscoelastic regime, and progressively weakens upon heating toward the liquid state. These results establish a direct connection between local steric frustration and thermally activated mechanical response, showing that thermal nonaffine metrics and thermal strain fields provide a physically transparent bridge between local structure and thermally driven rearrangements in amorphous glasses.
