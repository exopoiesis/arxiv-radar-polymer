# Energetics of Coherent Heterophase Interfaces: Separating Chemical and Strain Contributions

**Authors:** Adolfo Salgado-Casanova, Jacob Pietryga, Yi Xia, Chris Wolverton

Coherent interfacial energies govern the formation and evolution of multiphase microstructures, but their calculation is complicated by the elastic energy required to accommodate lattice mismatch. Here, we construct a high-throughput DFT database of 365 coherent FCC/FCC, BCC/BCC, and HCP/HCP interfaces involving 41 metallic elements and lattice mismatches of up to 25%. We use the supercell approach, consisting of constructing superlattices of different thicknesses to separate the coherency strain energy from the chemical contribution to the interfacial energy. We classify the results according to the long-range ordering tendency of each binary system and the sign of the calculated interfacial energy. Most phase-separating systems exhibit positive interfacial energies, whereas most compound-ordering systems exhibit negative values, reflecting favorable unlike-atom bonding. Exceptions show that the chemical preference at a particular interface need not coincide with bulk phase behavior: coherency strain can oppose ordering, and stable compounds can adopt structures distinct from the calculated superlattices. No clear correlation of interfacial energy with lattice mismatch is observed, whereas coherency strain energy increases with mismatch. For phase-separating systems, the lowest interfacial energies generally occur along the closest-packed orientations, while this trend is absent for ordering systems. Comparisons with reported interfacial energies and binary phase diagrams show qualitative consistency, although quantitative agreement varies with reference states, structural constraints, and temperature. The database provides a systematic reference for metallic coherent heterophase interfaces and demonstrates the importance of separating strain and chemical energy contributions when interpreting alloy behavior.
