# Universal Multichannel Coherence-Mediated Thermal Transport in Carbon Allotropes: Experimental Evidence and Scaling Laws

**Authors:** A. Jeżowski, A. I. Krivchikov, O. A. Korolyuk, V. V. Sagan, Y. V. Horbatenko, S. V. Cherednychenko, A. V. Dolbin, N. A. Vinnikov, O. Bezkrovnyi, D. Szewczyk

Understanding thermal transport in structurally disordered carbon materials remains a fundamental challenge because conventional models based on a single coherence contribution cannot accurately describe heat conduction over a broad temperature range. In this work, the thermal conductivity of graphene oxide (GO), thermally reduced graphene oxide (trGO), GO-hydrated fullerene composites (GO-HyFn), expanded graphite (EG), and EG-multiwalled carbon nanotube (MWCNT) composites was measured between 4.5 and 300 K. Despite substantial differences in density, porosity, graphitic ordering, and microstructure, all materials exhibit glass-like temperature dependence of thermal conductivity.   The experimental results demonstrate that the conventional two-channel model, comprising a Peierls contribution and a single thermally activated coherence term, systematically fails to reproduce the observed temperature dependence. To resolve this discrepancy, we propose a generalized multichannel phenomenological model in which the coherence contribution consists of up to three Arrhenius-type channels with distinct activation energies. Depending on the structural state, two or three coherence channels are required to accurately describe the thermal conductivity.   A key finding is the reproducible hierarchy of activation energies across diverse carbon allotropes, following approximately (E2 ~ 4E1 and E3 ~ (12-25)E1). While the channel amplitudes increase with material density, the activation energies remain nearly invariant, indicating universal vibrational energy scales. The proposed framework also consistently describes published data for other disordered carbon materials. These results provide experimental evidence for multichannel coherence-mediated heat transport and establish a unified phenomenological bridge between structural hierarchy with thermal transport in disordered carbon solids.
