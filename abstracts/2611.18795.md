# Lower Bound of Thermal Conductivity in Graphene

**Authors:** Yan Gao, Yafei Ding, Hongbin Xu, Zhaoxuan Tang, Fuqing Duan, Wenmei Luo, Zhensheng Zhang, Xiaohang Zhang, Guimei Zhu, Yahui Xue, Baowen Li

The intrinsic thermal conductivity ($κ$) of graphene remains highly controversial because of physical and metrological complexities. Although the thermal bridge method enables reliable measurements, sample fabrication remains challenging. Here, we develop a highly efficient patterning and transfer technique to systematically fabricate and characterize suspended bilayer graphene (BLG) thermal bridges with lengths of 1-30 $μ$m. Suspended BLG with unavoidable polymer residues exhibits length-independent thermal conductivity of approximately 570 W/(m K) at room temperature, remaining nearly constant from 250 to 350 K and robust to rigorous surface cleaning and amorphous silicon nitride substrates. Homogeneous non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (HNEMD) simulations show that amorphous carbon coverage of only approximately 0.01 monolayer is sufficient to suppress out-of-plane flexural acoustic (ZA) phonons. These findings challenge the prevailing view that rigid substrates suppress ZA phonons more strongly than surface contamination and identify a robust lower bound of $κ$ governed by amorphous surface scattering. This lower bound reflects heat conduction in graphene under realistic device conditions and provides a reference for fundamental phonon transport studies and the integration of graphene into thermal management applications in electronic devices and chips.
