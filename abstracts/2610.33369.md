# Contact-Free Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Mechanophenotyping by Amplitude-Modulated Ultrasound and Phase-Locked Optical Diffraction Tomography: Computational Feasibility and Initial Experimental Readouts

**Authors:** Yagmur Isik, Merve Sevgi, Esmahan Caglar, Aysenur Akgun, Hasan Berkay Abdioglu, Gokhan Bora Esmer, Huseyin Uvet

We investigate whether acoustic stimulation combined with phase-locked optical diffraction tomography (ODT) could measure the mechanical responses of the nucleus and cytoplasm separately in adherent cells. A megahertz ultrasound carrier is amplitude-modulated at low frequencies, while angle-scanned holography provides a route to reconstruct the first-harmonic refractive-index response in three dimensions. We built a computational model connecting viscoelastic finite-element simulations of MDA-MB-231 cells with a refractive-index phantom, hologram formation, camera noise and ODT reconstruction. Under an assumed 0.1 Pa harmonic shear traction at 10 Hz, the model predicts 3.6 to 9.2 nm of nuclear shear and 6.6 to 15.1 nm of apical motion. The nuclear-to-cytoplasmic amplitude ratio (R_A) is primarily sensitive to stiffness contrast in the tested scenarios, while the relative phase (Delta phi) responds to differences in dissipation. A simulated protocol using 100 illumination angles and 16 phase visits per angle recovered an imposed stiffening and recovery trajectory after averaging four acquisitions. We also obtained initial 3D tomograms of adherent MDA-MB-231 cells and report preliminary regional measurements from five cells. Their mean amplitude ratio was 0.616 plus or minus 0.098, and their mean relative phase was -1.91 plus or minus 0.58 degrees (mean and between-cell sample standard deviation). All five amplitude ratios were below unity, and all phase differences were negative, consistent with the qualitative directions of selected model scenarios. The small cohort and the absence of independently quantified experimental phase noise prevent a claim of quantitative mechanical validation. Together, these results support further evaluation of phase-locked acoustic ODT and identify experimental requirements for a controlled validation study.
