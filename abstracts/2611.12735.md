# Catalysis-induced phase separation of enzymes via diffusivity contrast

**Authors:** Marie Jardat, Jacques Fries, Vincent Dahirel, Jaime Agudo-Canalejo, Pierre Illien

Catalysis can drive phase separation by coupling local chemical composition to molecular transport, even in the absence of attractive interactions. Here, we demonstrate such catalysis-induced phase separation in a microscopic particle-based model of enzymes catalyzing the interconversion of substrate and product molecules with different diffusivities. We derive an analytical instability criterion in the dilute limit, showing that a homogeneous mixture becomes unstable when the nonequilibrium chemical drive and the diffusivity contrast are sufficiently large, and the substrate and product concentration exceeds a threshold set by the enzyme volume. Brownian-dynamics simulations confirm the predicted instability and reveal macroscopic coexistence of enzyme-rich and enzyme-poor phases, including in regimes where equilibrium depletion interactions cannot account for demixing. Finally, we show that, upon eliminating the fast chemical degrees of freedom, the reactive ternary mixture maps onto an effective binary mixture nonreciprocal interactions. Our results establish an explicit microscopic mechanism for catalysis-induced phase separation and connect chemically driven phase separation to the physics of motility-regulated and nonreciprocal matter.
