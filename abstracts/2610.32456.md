# Side-Chain Engineering Controls Molecular Packing and Vertical Electron Transport in Discrete n-Type NDI-T2 Oligomers

**Authors:** Zihao Liu, Hajar Komekoz, Nisarg Trivedi, Max Dorfner, Lokesh Rasabathina, Rico Ehrler, Mike Hambsch, Chen Wang, Dietrich R. T. Zahn, Jörg Matysik, Stefan C. B. Mannsfeld, Carsten Deibel, Olav Hellwig, Frank Ortmann, Michael Sommer, Georgeta Salvan

High-efficiency vertical charge transport in multilayer organic optoelectronic devices highly depends on molecular structure and arrangement. Using two T2-(NDI-T2)2 conjugated oligomers with ethylhexyl (EH) and octyldodecyl (OD) side chains as a model system, their effects on electron transport are investigated using, among other techniques, atomic force microscopy (AFM), conductive atomic force microscopy (C-AFM), grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS), steady-state optical spectroscopy, and time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) calculations. The oligomers of both materials exhibit relatively disordered structures in the as-cast state, whereas annealing enhances structural order and leads to side-chain-dependent changes in molecular stacking. EH-based films show a smaller annealing-induced photoluminescence shift than OD-based films, suggesting less pronounced stacking and conformational rearrangements, consistent with TDDFT-supported exciton analysis. The current-voltage characteristics are analyzed using the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model. After annealing, both materials exhibit improved vertical electron mobility. The EH-based films reach a maximum mobility of 1.2 x 10^-4 cm^2 V^-1 s^-1. The combined results suggest that the shorter EH side chains modify the out-of-plane packing environment and facilitate vertical electron transport. These results establish a structure-property relationship between side-chain structure, molecular order, exciton coupling and vertical mobility, providing a molecular design basis for optimising organic semiconductors through side-chain engineering.
