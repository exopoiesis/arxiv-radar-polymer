# Tuning fluid frictional drag through flow-induced aggregation of wormlike micelles

**Authors:** Lucas Warwaruk, Amanda Tiano, Berith Isaac, Gareth H. McKinley

Charged amphiphilic surfactants are versatile polymorphic molecules which can form supramolecular structures, such as wormlike micelles. We study the rheology and non-Newtonian fluid dynamics of the cationic surfactant trimethyltetradecylammonium chloride (TTAC) combined with the aromatic counter-ion sodium salicylate (NaSal). In the non-dilute regime, this solution exhibits a linear viscoelastic response consistent with a single-mode Jeffreys model, and in capillary pinch-off experiments, fluid filaments form extended cylindrical threads, indicating strong strain-stiffening. In the dilute regime the linear viscoelasticity and extensional rheology become immeasurably small, but the TTAC/NaSal mixture develops flow-induced structures at high shear rates, as the result of an end-to-end concatenation of micelles produced by shear-induced flow alignment. These flow-induced structures augment both the measured viscosity and the viscous drag in laminar flows at high shear rates (by as much as 50 %). In turbulent Taylor-Couette flow, the flow-induced structures produce a sudden and dramatic destabilizing influence on the stable toroidal Taylor vortices, resulting in upwards of a 200 % enhancement in turbulent drag. However, with increasing Reynolds number, the turbulent flow transitions smoothly to a state of drag reduction, generating up to a 30 % reduction in fluid frictional drag but without the shear-induced degradation that plagues drag reduction strategies based on solutions of high molecular weight polymer, such as polyacrylamide. These results show that surfactants are a unique and effective modifier of fluid frictional drag in both laminar and turbulent flow regimes, capable of producing both significant levels of drag enhancement and drag reduction by tuning the composition and operating conditions.
