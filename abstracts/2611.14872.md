# Soft material mechanical property degradation at high strain rates

**Authors:** Victor Sanchez, Joseph Beckett, Aryan Shah, Sicong Wang, Jin Yang, Jonathan B. Estrada, Mauro Rodriguez

The rheometry of soft, tissue-like materials at ultra-high strain rates is critical to biomechanics, materials science, and medical engineering. One approach for characterizing high-rate material response is Inertial Microcavitation Rheometry (IMR), which infers viscoelastic constitutive behavior from the measured radius-time history of a laser-induced cavitation bubble. At these rates, materials may exhibit history-dependent behavior, including damage, fracture, and plasticity. However, conventional IMR analyses typically assume constant material properties throughout the cavitation event, limiting their ability to capture such changes. We present an IMR framework for identifying effective time varying material properties from measured cavitation radius histories. The framework allows the shear modulus, viscosity, or both to evolve during the event using normalized sigmoid histories. Synthetic tests recover prescribed constant and time varying material histories. Application to polyacrylamide and gelatin datasets yields effective material-property histories across formulations and experimental conditions. Viscosity decreased across the datasets, while the extent of shear modulus reduction varied with material and formulation. When both properties decreased, their reductions could occur at different stages of cavitation. This distinction in mechanical property degradation depends on both the property and its evolution during high-strain events.
