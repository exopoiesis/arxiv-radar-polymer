# Re-emergence of fingering in solidifying liquid-metal drops

**Authors:** Siyu Zhang, Feng Wang, Yuan Ma, Hao Jiang, Kai Leong Chong, Quan Zhou, Chao Sun, Jianhua Zhang

Solidification is expected to suppress fingering by removing mobile liquid. Here we report an unexpected re-emergence of fingering in impacting molten-tin drops: cooling first weakens peripheral fingers, but stronger undercooling restores them. At weak undercooling, fingers originate from a decelerating liquid rim and solidification reduces the effective acceleration that drives Rayleigh--Taylor-like amplification. At strong undercooling, solidification structures develop while liquid is still spreading, creating lateral mobility contrasts that redirect the residual flow. Comparison between the kinematically estimated solidification time and the experimentally measured fingering-onset and growth-termination times reveals a reversal in temporal ordering across the temperature range. A physics-based reduced-order model combines solidification-modified rim deceleration with mobility-gradient growth, captures the characteristic onset dynamics and accounts for the final finger length within the same physical framework.These findings establish solidification as an active means of regulating interfacial flow and morphology, with broad implications spanning metal-based additive manufacturing and debris mitigation in semiconducting industry.
