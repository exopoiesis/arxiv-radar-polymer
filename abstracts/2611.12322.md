# Ultrafast Inter-Aggregate Energy Transfer Drives Efficient Charge Generation in Organic Homojunctions

**Authors:** Shanchao Ouyang, Hang Yuen Ho, Sudhi Mahadevan, Ziyao Yue, Haochen Liu, Zhiqiang Guan, Chun-Sing Lee, Shu Kong So, Sai-Wing Tsang

Y6-based homojunction organic photovoltaic devices have recently achieved exceptional power conversion efficiencies, surpassing those of conventional organic photovoltaic materials. Although unusually long exciton diffusion lengths and efficient exciton dissociation have been reported, the fundamental mechanism underlying this outstanding performance remains under debate. Here, we report that structurally distinct aggregates in Y6 thin films exhibit complementary charge-transfer (CT) characters that enable directional energy transfer and enhanced exciton delocalization. Using ultrafast spectroscopy, we identify highly efficient Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) between Y6 aggregates, with an ensemble-averaged efficiency of 65% occurring on a sub-picosecond timescale (<1 ps). This aggregate-mediated energy-transfer pathway drives long-range energy migration in Y6 thin films and accounts for the remarkable external quantum efficiencies observed in homojunction devices, even in the absence of conventional donor-acceptor bulk heterojunction morphologies and despite substantial fractions of weak-CT components. Here, using Y6 as a model non-fullerene acceptor (NFA) system, we show that structurally distinct aggregates coexisting in NFA thin films possess complementary CT characters, which are expected to enable directional energy transfer and enhanced exciton delocalization. We establish a general multi-component FRET framework that quantitatively links molecular aggregation, CT character, and excited-state dynamics in systems with multiple packing motifs. This work provides insight into the development of next-generation high-performance organic electronic materials and devices.
