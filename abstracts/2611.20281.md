# Resolvent-based modeling of turbulent flow over multilayer compliant surfaces

**Authors:** Rutvij Bhagwat, Kourosh Shoele

Compliant coatings offer a passive approach to modifying near-wall turbulence and potentially reducing skin-friction drag, but high-fidelity exploration of the coupled parameter space is costly. We develop a two-way coupled resolvent-Galerkin framework for turbulent channel flow over finite-thickness, layered viscoelastic coatings. The fluid is modeled using the Navier-Stokes equations linearized about a turbulent mean state, while the solid is represented by a through-thickness Galerkin discretization of the elastodynamic equations. The solid response is condensed into a spectral surface-impedance operator at the fluid-solid interface. At $Re_τ= 180$, stiff panels approach the rigid-wall response, whereas more compliant panels modify the low-rank response associated with the near-wall cycle. A Reynolds-number study shows that panels with fixed nondimensional material stiffness can produce different responses depending on whether their thickness scales with inner or outer flow length scales. The influence of viscoelastic damping is examined across low- and high-frequency regimes, where stiffness-proportional damping alters the high-frequency fractional-energy distribution, while the low-frequency distribution remains relatively insensitive to damping. For multilayer panels, the fluid-facing material determines the interfacial wave speed, while the subsurface stiffness distribution modifies the panel's effective thickness. Temporal stability analysis identifies regions of parameter space where a steady harmonic response is relevant and regions where instabilities grow. Finally, a leading-order, covariance-based drag-estimation framework relates the coupled response to changes in the mean flow and to the estimated skin-friction drag. Together, these tools provide a basis for screening coating designs and selecting candidates for high-fidelity simulations and experiments.
