# Snap-Through Symmetry Breaking in Multicellular Aggregates

**Authors:** Martí Planasdemunt-Hospital, David Oriola

Symmetry breaking is a fundamental process in biological organization. Yet the physical mechanisms that control its onset in multicellular systems remain poorly understood. Motivated by gastruloids -- multicellular aggregates in which cells differentiate and spontaneously break symmetry -- we study a two-phase viscoelastic solid drop as a minimal model of tissue polarization. We show that elastocapillary competition fundamentally alters the classical engulfing transition of fluid drops. While symmetry breaking occurs continuously in the purely viscous limit, bulk elasticity instead leads to a discontinuous snap-like transition. We demonstrate that this behavior can be understood as a wetting transition governed by a spreading coefficient which depends on the drop size and phase volume fraction. Applying our model to gastruloids, we find a critical volume fraction for symmetry breaking, providing a self-organized mechanism for robust developmental timing. Our results identify a generic elastocapillary transition that enables spontaneous symmetry breaking in biphasic drops.
