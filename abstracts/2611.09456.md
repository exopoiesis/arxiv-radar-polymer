# Increased Crystal Nucleation in the Glassy State of a Polymer/Water Mixture

**Authors:** Mika Kobayashi, Shingo Kobayashi, Kazuyoshi Shiga, Hiroaki Yamada, Masaru Tanaka

Within classical nucleation theory, crystal nucleation requires sufficient supersaturation, but is suppressed at low temperatures due to reduced molecular mobility, leading to a maximum nucleation rate typically above the glass transition temperature $T_g$. Here, we experimentally show that this conventional picture breaks down in a biocompatible polymer poly(2-methoxyethyl acrylate)(PMEA)/water mixture. We find that the number of nuclei increases dramatically below $T_g$, with the effective nucleation rate exhibiting an unusual maximum at approximately $T_g-10$ K. Calorimetric experiments reveal a strong temperature-history dependence of cold crystallization of water in hydrated PMEA, which is explained by the observed nucleation behavior. We propose that the unusual enhancement of nucleation below $T_g$ provides a key mechanism linking high glass-forming ability and cold crystallization upon heating, thereby explaining the striking asymmetry in crystallization behavior between cooling and heating. Our findings suggest that a hidden nucleation-rate maximum below $T_g$ may also underlie cold crystallization in other glass-forming liquids.
