# New Closed-Form Approximations in Statistical Mechanics Obtained by Symbolic Regression

**Authors:** Omkar Kunjir, Alina Popova, Rasul Abdusalamov, Mikhail Itskov

Obtaining accurate and analytically tractable closed-form expressions for complex physical systems remains a fundamental challenge in statistical mechanics. In this work, we investigate three approximation problems in polymer statistical mechanics using two complementary symbolic regression frameworks: Deep Symbolic Optimization (DSO), based on reinforcement learning, and PyOperon, based on genetic programming with nonlinear least-squares constant optimization.   First, for the force-extension relationship of the Worm-Like Chain (WLC) model, DSO achieves a maximum relative error below 0.15%, while PyOperon reduces it to 0.05%, outperforming the Marko-Siggia formula (approximately 17%) and the Petrosyan approximation. Second, for the logarithmic factorial, ln(n!), both frameworks recover the dominant structure of Stirling's formula directly from analytical data. Using residual learning on a continued-fraction expansion of the Stirling remainder, PyOperon reduces the maximum relative error by a factor of 1.3 x 10^4 compared with the most accurate of nine classical approximations for n in [1, 30]. Third, for the non-Gaussian radial probability density of the Freely Jointed Chain (FJC) model, physics-informed residual learning improves the approximation by factors of 11.2 over Darabi et al. (2023) and 4.6 over the steepest-descent approximation.   The discovered expressions remain analytically tractable across all three applications. These results demonstrate the complementary capabilities of DSO and PyOperon for discovering accurate closed-form approximations in statistical and continuum mechanics.
