# How many events does entanglement-based positronium imaging need? Information limits of Compton polarimetry with CZT detectors

**Authors:** Haitao Zhou, Ruonan Wang, Jianyong Hu, Zhifang Wu, Sijin Li

The degree of polarisation correlation of the two annihilation photons, measured by Compton polarimetry, has been proposed as a PET biomarker of tissue microstructure, but the statistics it requires have not been quantified. We parameterise the measurement by the linear-polarisation correlation coefficient $κ$ ($κ=1$ maximally entangled, $1/2$ separable), derive the classical Fisher information of double-Compton events and compare it with the quantum Fisher information, relate $κ$ to the annihilation channels of positronium in tissue, and simulate pixelated CdZnTe (CZT) rings. The model is checked against three published measurements and an independent GATE/Geant4 simulation. A double-Compton event carries at most 0.12-0.14 units of Fisher information about $κ$ and 0.015 on average, 0.1-0.6% of the quantum limit. Event-wise maximum likelihood reaches the Cramer-Rao bound, whereas the conventional ratio R needs 1.2-2.3 times more events. The observed coefficient is $κ_{obs}=1-α(1-κ_{pick})$, with a pick-off share $α$ of 0.13-0.29 in tissue; it is sensitive to lipid fraction but not to oxygen, and carries no tissue information if pick-off photons are maximally entangled. Published porous-polymer data imply $κ_{pick}$ = 0.34-0.58. In a 15-mm CZT module, ordering ambiguity, position errors and electron transport roughly halve the information per event, and sequence reconstruction of multi-site events recovers this loss. With $^{18}$F and an eight-module ring, $κ$ can be measured to $\pm0.01$ in a 10-mm tumour, but separating tissues by $κ$ alone requires 6-74 times more decays than are available.
