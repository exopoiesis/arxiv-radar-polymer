# Targeted material doping to enable the formation and coherent propagation of Laser Induced Periodic Surface Structures (LIPSS): the ZnO paradigm

**Authors:** Gonzalo Gomez-Munoz, Rocio Ariza, Juan Francisco Ramos-Justicia, Fatima Cabello Pardos, Antonio Pablo Pérez-Marín, Jose Gonzalo, Belén Sotillo, Binbing Xia, Paloma Fernandez, Javier Solis

This work explores how the initial free carrier density governs the formation and coherent propagation of Low Spatial Frequency Laser-Induced Periodic Surface Structures (LSF-LIPSS). As a paradigm we have analyzed the behavior of ad-hoc-doped ZnO films. ZnO is a multifunctional Transparent Conducting Oxide (TCO) with many scientific and technologic uses including applications in biomedicine, photonics, sensing or photocatalysis. Many of them would benefit from the use of large area, electrically anisotropic surfaces. By introducing small amounts of Yb3+ as donor dopant in ZnO films grown by Pulse Laser Deposition (PLD), we have substantially improved its carrier density at room temperature (RT) and its electrical conductivity while preserving the functional properties of pure ZnO. For RT carrier densities ne > 1019 cm-3, coherent propagation of LSF-LIPSS has been successfully achieved upon irradiation with 350 fs laser pulses at 1030 nm at high repetition rate (500 kHz) and for laser scanning speeds well above 1 m/s. However, the ablation mechanism (ZnO sublimation at the LIPSS valleys) and the large melting temperature of ZnO, make the thermal conductivity of the substrate, the film thickness and the irradiation pulse duration to play a crucial role in determining the morphology of the LSF-LIPPS propagated over large areas, especially in avoiding film cracking. The results achieved upon optimization fully confirm the role of free carrier density in the large-scale propagation of high quality LSF-LIPSS in doped ZnO and enable using these structures to easily generate large electrical anisotropies over macroscopic regions with conductivities along the LIPSS several times larger than transversally.
