# 3D-VASE: Volumetric-Aware Scalable and Efficient Multi-Layer Design Synthesis for 3D-Printed Microfluidics

**Authors:** Yushen Zhang, Jiashuo Chen, Tsun-Ming Tseng, Ulf Schlichtmann

Additive manufacturing (3D printing) enables complex multi-layer microfluidic devices, but the physical synthesis of passive-flow designs remains a key bottleneck. In such systems, designers must encode timing synchronization and target dosing directly into channel geometry, even though the available routing space across multiple 3D layers is often highly congested, which makes scaling to complex designs difficult. Existing approaches formulate placement, routing, and fluidic constraints as a single monolithic optimization, leading to combinatorial explosion and poor performance on complex designs. This paper presents a volumetric-aware two-stage synthesis framework that decouples global structure from routing detail. A Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) model first computes a compact multi-layer placement, followed by a dynamic 3D routing engine that enforces timing and volumetric constraints during path construction. To further improve routability in dense layouts, we introduce a hierarchical conflict resolution strategy combining local rip-up-and-reroute with controlled global expansion. Experimental results show that, under timing and volumetric constraints, 3D-VASE consistently improves synthesis speed and, in most test cases, also improves layout quality. Notably, for difficult large-scale cases where prior methods often failed to return any solution within the time limit, 3D-VASE produces feasible high-quality layouts within practical runtimes. These advances enable fast, iterative design-space exploration for complex 3D-printed microfluidic systems.
