# The exponent $3/4$ for the exceptional sets of the equations $K/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$

**Authors:** Dmytro Trybushnyi

For $K\ge4$ let $E_K(N)$ be the number of integers $2\le n\le N$ for which $K/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ has no solution in positive integers; $K=4$ is the Erdős-Straus equation. We prove that $E_K(N)\le N\exp(-c((\log N)^3/K)^{1/4})$ with absolute constants $c,B>0$, uniformly for $4\le K\le(\log N)^3/B$. This improves the exponent $2/3$ of Vaughan's bound of 1970. The bound is non-trivial for $K$ up to a constant times $(\log N)^3$, which is, up to a factor $(\log\log N)^{2+o(1)}$, the scale at which, by work of Pomerance and Weingartner, exceptional primes become common. The sieve is combinatorial: a truncated inclusion-exclusion over the classes of parametric solutions, whose second moment is the partition function of a two-copy polymer gas, bounded by a real form of the Kotecký-Preiss criterion. Averaging over the residue classes of the small primes gives the family its full mass, and summing each cluster in a random order bounds the cluster sums. Since a divisor of an exception is an exception, nothing about the small primes themselves is needed, and the sieve can use the classes of the whole family of parametric solutions, one triple per class. All constants are explicit, and the whole proof has been checked in the proof assistant Lean 4.
