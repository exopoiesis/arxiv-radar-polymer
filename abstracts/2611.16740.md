# Near-Threshold Kink Trapping Enhances Defect Healing in an Adaptive $\boldsymbol{φ^4}$ Chain

**Authors:** Sh. Minapoor, Sergey V. Dmitriev, A. Moradi Marjaneh

We study the scattering of topological kinks in a discrete $φ^4$ chain with a localized, adaptive defect, motivated by autonomous self-healing mechanisms in fibrous and polymeric chains. The defect represents damaged flanking bonds whose coupling stiffness evolves dynamically via an internal healing variable, driven by the local energy density and moderated by a viscoelastic relaxation rate $β$. Using high-resolution numerical simulations, we identify a sharp transition at a critical impact velocity $v_c$ separating kink reflection from transmission ($v_c \simeq 0.4183$ for the benchmark initial defect stiffness $C_{\min} = 0.2$). While subcritical kinks ($v_i < v_c$) bounce back with little energy deposition and supercritical ones ($v_i > v_c$) cross the defect too quickly to induce noticeable recovery, kinks near the threshold ($v_i \simeq v_c$) undergo resonant trapping. This prolonged dwell time transfers substantial vibrational energy into the defect, driving the healing variable to nearly full restoration ($h_{\max} \approx 0.98$). Notably, while $v_c$ and the peak defect energy $E_{d,\max}$ depend strongly on $C_{\min}$ and are largely unaffected by $β$, non-zero relaxation ($β> 0$) shortens the trapping window and reduces the final recovery efficiency. Our results show that energy-driven self-healing in discrete lattices is controlled by the cumulative dwell time and time-integrated energy excitation rather than the instantaneous impact peak alone.
