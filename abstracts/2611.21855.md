# Shortest-path percolation and structural robustness of quantum internet networks

**Authors:** Victor H. F. Francheto, Thomas Peron

We study shortest-path percolation on spatial networks that model the quantum internet based on optical fibers. In this process, the removal of paths describes the consumption of the entangled links used for communication. We compare finite and unrestricted path-length bounds for networks whose average degree either increases with system size or remains approximately constant. We show that networks become more robust as the average degree increases, whereas networks with approximately constant average degree exhibit a data collapse for the fixed finite bounds considered. Using Monte Carlo sampling of the shortest-path-length distribution, we estimate an equivalent path cost, a finite bound that approximately reproduces the degradation of the network in the unrestricted case. For networks with fixed average degree, this cost grows approximately as the square root of the number of nodes. Increasing the bound beyond this value requires additional computation time without appreciably changing the percolation dynamics. As a complementary result, for Erdős-Rényi random networks, we find an empirical relation between the initial average degree and the approximately common crossing point of the percolation curves for different path-length bounds.
