Mechano-chemical control of enzymatic activity in crowded environments

Michalis Chatzittofi, Luca Cocconi, Jakob Metson, Ramin Golestanian

arXiv:2611.03239·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-02

Macromolecular crowding has long been known to alter biochemical equilibria and enzyme kinetics, with reported catalytic activities that may increase, decrease, or vary non-monotonically with crowder concentration. Classical Michaelis--Menten kinetics does not by itself identify which of these responses should occur for a conformationally active enzyme. Here, we develop a minimal non-equilibrium mechano-chemical theory for a fueled enzyme embedded in a bath of passive crowders and uncover a fluctuation-induced effective force dipole acting on the enzyme subunits, akin to a generalized form of dispersion or depletion interaction. Since the preferred conformation of the model enzyme varies through the catalytic cycle, the fluctuation-induced mechanical load affects the relevant kinetic activation barriers and is sufficient to produce both monotonic inhibition and non-monotonic modification of turnover. The framework provides a thermodynamically consistent route for linking crowded environments to catalytic currents, suggesting how mechanical design principles could be leveraged to control enzymatic pathways.
