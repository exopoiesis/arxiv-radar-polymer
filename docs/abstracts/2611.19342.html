Tunable topological droplets on supercoiled DNA under tension

Enrico Skoruppa, Prithwidip Saha, Jan Brugués, Jan Lipfert, Davide Marenduzzo, Helmut Schiessel

arXiv:2611.19342·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-14

DNA--protein co-condensation is an important regulatory mechanism for gene expression and repair. For straight DNA under constant stretching forces, co-condensation is ``all-or-nothing'': above a critical force, condensates are prevented from nucleating, while below it the entire molecule collapses into a droplet. Here we show by coarse-grained simulations and analytic theory that DNA supercoiling mechanically differentiates the condensation substrate: DNA plectonemes constitute force-insulated pockets on which condensates can nucleate even above the critical force. The size of the resulting droplets is tunable through the applied force and linking number. Surface tension drives the coarsening of multiple plectoneme-associated condensates into a single topological droplet. Magnetic tweezers experiments with spermine reproduce both the force-dependent collapse and the supercoiling-dependent condensation.
