On the thermal behavior of composites embedding extended general interfaces

Soheil Firooz, Kashish Veda Eluri, Ali Javili, Paul Steinmann

arXiv:2611.24414·math-ph·Published 2026-11-18

In heterogeneous materials, surface roughness, chemical incompatibility, mechanical mismatch or manufacturing defects result in imperfect bonding between the constituents. Such imperfections introduce a finite-thickness interphase zone between the constituents that possess a graded or inhomogeneous micro-structure. It is virtually impossible to resolve the behavior of such interphases. Instead, a well-established approach is to replace the interphase by a zero-thickness interface endowed with its own energetic structure. Conventional interface models define the interface temperature as an average of the temperatures of the surrounding bulk phases. However, this assumption is only adequate to represent uniform homogeneous interphases and it fails to accurately capture the behavior of graded or inhomogeneous interphases. In this work, we address this limitation by allowing for unequal contributions of the surrounding bulk phases to the interface temperature. We systematically revisit the resulting balance equations and present the computational framework for the finite element implementation of the model. We further incorporate the proposed model within the homogenization framework and derive closed-form analytical expressions for the effective thermal conductivity of fiber- and particle-reinforced composites, accouting for the influence of the extended general interface. Finally, through a series of numerical examples, we demonstrate the capabilities of the proposed model and investigate the influence of its parameters on the size-dependent thermal behavior of composites.
