Disentangling Lengthscales in Disordered Fracture

Matthaios Chouzouris, Marcelo A. Dias

arXiv:2611.27581·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-20

The interplay between quenched disorder and collective interactions can generate multiple emergent lengthscales in damage processes, yet their roles in determining the strength of heterogeneous materials remain incompletely understood. Here, we develop a coarse-grained description of load redistribution in the two-dimensional random fuse model (RFM) with Weibull-distributed quenched disorder, interpreting shielding in the crack-tip damage density as a consequence of nonlocal current redistribution. Our numerical results distinguish this emergent screening length, identified as the size of the statistical fracture process zone, from the regularizing length in Bazant's established quasibrittle size-effect law (SEL), revealing their distinct disorder dependences, and challenging previous associations of the two. We further uncover a comparable scaling between the SEL and pre-failure crack extension, suggesting the former may instead be more naturally associated with a percolation process of micro-fractures around the crack-tip. These findings disentangle important emergent lengthscales within the RFM, and highlight a candidate physical mechanism for explaining macroscopic size effects in quasibrittle fracture.
