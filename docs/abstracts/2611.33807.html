Controlling truncation errors in loop tensor network renormalization

Rikuto Fushio, Kokin Cho, Yuta Yamamoto, Akira Otsuki, Masahito Yamazaki

arXiv:2611.33807·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-25

At finite bond dimension $χ$, tensor network renormalization (TNR) of a critical model eventually becomes gapped. For LoopTNR with $O(2)$ symmetry on the $c=1$ critical line of the six-vertex model, parametrized by the Luttinger parameter $K$, we show that the correlation length $ξ$ of the gapped phase is set by the seed of the dimerization injected by the truncation, and depends on $χ$ only through it. Read from a weak-coupling window early in the same run, the seed predicts $ξ=10^3$-$10^{16}$ via the sine-Gordon mass-coupling relation with no free parameter, to within a few percent for $K\le1.5$ and about 10% at $K=1.75$; since the seed fluctuates strongly with $χ$, $ξ$ is not monotonic in $χ$. An alternation of the vertex weights shifts the seed; with its sign read from a $π$ twist, one short calibration predicts a counterterm that delays the breakdown in 16 of the 17 compensated groups of a campaign fixed in advance. A correction of the coarse-grained tensors, repeated during the flow, keeps the runs at $K=1$ and $1.5$ critical up to step 160, the last step computed, and exposes the marginal truncation error that remains. Our findings illustrate a systematic procedure for controlling the relevant truncation error, and thereby extending the critical window, in tensor network renormalization at finite bond dimension.
