Solids activated by multi-body nonreciprocity

Brady Wu, Daniel S. Seara, Qinghao Mao, Vincenzo Vitelli, Heinrich M. Jaeger

arXiv:2611.30058·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-23

Field-mediated forces between particles can be both nonreciprocal and irreducibly multi-body, giving rise to emergent activity in systems of individually passive particles. While prior work extensively studied how multi-body nonreciprocity can activate few-body systems, fuel intermittency, and drive liquid-like behavior, the effect on the solid state of large particle ensembles is less explored. Here we use two-dimensional, close-packed rafts of identical, athermal, acoustically levitated microspheres to show how multi-body nonreciprocity injects activity in solids. At the level of both individual particles and collective dynamics, crystalline rafts exhibit persistent circulations with no net handedness. The rafts also exhibit a phonon-like dispersion relation corresponding to a large Poisson ratio, indicating an induced softness to shear. Within the bulk, lattice symmetry masks microscopic nonreciprocity, although it can manifest in renormalized, reciprocal elastic coefficients. At dislocations and raft edges, where this symmetry is broken, multi-body nonreciprocal interactions act as local generators of activity. A coarse-grained microscopic model of multi-body nonreciprocity, calibrated on three-particle simulations, recovers the rafts' experimentally observed dispersion relations and localized activity. Using these findings, we illustrate how to harness defect-induced activity for programmable movement by arranging microscopic dislocations to drive macroscopic propulsion and rotation of a whole raft.
