Quantum Control Geometry as a Metric of Chaos

Mahmoud Abdelshafy, Joaquin F. Rodriguez-Nieva

arXiv:2611.13101·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-09

Quantum-chaotic systems share the same coarse-grained features characteristic of random matrices, such as Wigner-Dyson level-spacing statistics, yet can exhibit strikingly different eigenstate structure and many-body dynamics, including relaxation, transport, scrambling, and entanglement growth. This raises the fundamental question of whether these finer-grained features probe genuinely distinct aspects of quantum chaos or, instead, reflect different manifestations of a common underlying structure---and whether such structure provides a meaningful basis for regarding one many-body quantum system as "more chaotic" than another. Here, we address this question by introducing a quantum geometric framework that quantifies the susceptibility of many-body eigenstates to physically allowed deformations of the Hamiltonian, as constrained by spatial locality, symmetries, and conservation laws. The resulting geometric susceptibility is quantitative, providing a metric of chaos beyond traditional binary diagnostics; intrinsic, as it depends on the manifold of allowed Hamiltonian deformations rather than on the way the system is probed; and unifying, as it directly connects eigenstate structure with dynamics. For canonical models of quantum chaos, we find that minimal geometric susceptibility, maximal eigenstate randomness, broad spectral response of local operators, and the suppression of nonhydrodynamic slow structure coalesce in the same region of Hamiltonian control space. This suggests that these seemingly distinct signatures of chaos are different manifestations of a common underlying geometric structure. Our framework provides a route toward understanding the limits of relaxation and scrambling under physical constraints; in applications, it offers a means to engineer strongly chaotic regimes in programmable quantum systems with restricted controls.
