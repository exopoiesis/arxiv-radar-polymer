Bulk CMSX-4 Single-Crystal Builds via Laser-Directed Energy Deposition: Epitaxial Stability and Microstructure Evolution

Divya Nalajala, Swapnil Bhure, R. Ajithkannan, Shubhang Galagali, Abhik Choudhury

arXiv:2611.16535·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-12

Single-crystal processing of Ni-based superalloys using laser-directed energy deposition (L-DED) is extremely challenging, given the interplay of a number of processing parameters. In this work, we develop strategies to maintain single-crystal growth in long cylindrical rod geometries by combining dynamic adjustment of laser power, informed by in-situ infrared thermal feedback, with a simulation-guided external cooling system. This combined approach ensures optimal heat extraction during additive manufacturing, maintaining near-steady-state conditions at the solidification interface and extending continuous epitaxial growth to nearly twice the build height achievable through process control alone. The developed strategy results in continuous single-crystal growth, as validated by electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). The steady-state nature of solidification is revealed through the near-constant primary dendritic arm spacing. Further, the heat-treatment is optimized by reducing the solutionizing time (by 47\%), resulting in faster eutectic dissolution. Comparison of the aged microstructures reveals a similar distribution of precipitates between the classical Bridgman- and L-DED-processed samples.
