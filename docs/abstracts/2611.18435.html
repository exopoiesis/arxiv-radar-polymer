Entanglement and information dynamics in charged dual-unitary circuits with impurities

Shachar Fraenkel, Colin Rylands

arXiv:2611.18435·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-13

Local parts of a closed, non-equilibrium quantum many-body system can reach stationarity through the buildup of entanglement between different regions. In this paper we investigate how this process is affected by the presence of a quantum impurity that breaks a global symmetry of the bulk. In particular, we study a charged dual-unitary quantum circuit perturbed by a symmetry-breaking, non-dual-unitary gate acting on a spatially fixed pair of sites. In this setup we calculate the entanglement entropy for a subsystem that does not contain the impurity, as well as the mutual information and logarithmic negativity for a bipartite subsystem that straddles the impurity. We obtain exact results in the early time regime for these quantities from the dual unitarity of the bulk system. Beyond this regime, we compute the predictions of the quasiparticle and entanglement membrane pictures, supporting them with exact calculations obtained by taking the corresponding limits of the bulk entangling power. We find that the presence of a symmetry-breaking impurity can lead to a richer behaviour of quantum relaxation, including non-monotonic evolution of the entanglement entropy, a modification of the long-time steady state, and persistent subsystem mutual information and entanglement negativity. This behaviour is understood in terms of long-range correlations generated by scattering from the impurity, and facilitated by the ballistic propagation of the conserved charges.
