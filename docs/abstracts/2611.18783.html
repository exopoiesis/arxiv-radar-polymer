Motility-induced phase separation drives aggregation in chemotactic systems

Letong Xu, Robert G. Endres

arXiv:2611.18783·q-bio.CB·Published 2026-11-12

We present a unified continuum framework that combines chemotactic feedback with motility-induced phase separation (MIPS) to investigate the physical origin of aggregation in self-secreting cell populations. While classical Keller-Segel theory attributes pattern formation to chemotactic instabilities, we show that this mechanism breaks down in systems with proportional density-dependent (autocrine) production and uptake: linear stability analysis reveals that chemical fluctuations decouple from density, eliminating chemotactic aggregation at leading order. In contrast, introducing density-dependent motility restores instability through a purely mechanical mechanism corresponding to MIPS. Our results demonstrate that chemotactic feedback does not alter the nature of this instability, but instead enhances its growth rate and accelerates aggregation. This unifies two central paradigms of biological self-organization and revises the role of chemotaxis in collective cell migration.
