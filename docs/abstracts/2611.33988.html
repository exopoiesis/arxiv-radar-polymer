Exploring Physical Mechanisms Underlying the Emergence of Collective Order

Yitong Cai, Ruizhe Yan, Dongrun Jian, Jie Su, Jin Wang

arXiv:2611.33988·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-25

Emergence of collective order from disordered constituents is a hallmark of active matter, and the Vicsek model (VM) serves as a paradigmatic system for its study. However, whether density-induced emergence in the VM differs between angular (intrinsic) and vectorial (extrinsic) noise, and what mechanisms govern the transitions, remain largely unexplored. Remarkably, we uncover that collective order emerges from a disordered state in a continuous-like manner under intrinsic noise but abruptly under extrinsic noise. Combining nonequilibrium landscape-flux theory with a coarse-grained mapping method, we show that the landscape evolves continuously from a single basin to two and back to one under intrinsic noise, but the two-basin landscape appears abruptly under extrinsic noise across the critical transition to flocking. The counterclockwise nonequilibrium flux sustains global circular motion, providing the driving force for reshaping the landscape. The mean flux and entropy production rate change steeply near the emergence threshold and exhibit pronounced peaks within the coexistence region, identifying them as the dynamical and thermodynamic origins of emergence. Our findings reveal a distinct abrupt emergence under extrinsic noise and offer deeper mechanistic insight, practical applications (e.g., locating phase boundaries and distinguishing transition types), and extensibility to other nonequilibrium collective systems.
