On the Abels--Garcke--Grün Model for Incompressible Two-Phase Flows with Unmatched Densities: Phase Separation and Rayleigh--Taylor Instability

Fei Jiang, Maoyin Lv, Hao Wu

arXiv:2611.12298·math.AP·Published 2026-11-09

Instability phenomena are ubiquitous in multi-fluid flows. For binary fluid mixtures, phase separation may occur under specific conditions, in which a homogeneous state becomes unstable and spontaneously segregates into different phases, leading to the formation of distinct patterns. Furthermore, when a heavier fluid is superposed over a lighter one in a gravitational field, the fluid interface can be sensitive to perturbations that trigger the Rayleigh--Taylor (RT) instability, i.e., the lighter fluid penetrates upward into the heavier fluid against gravity. We investigate such instability phenomena and the inhibiting conditions for incompressible, immiscible two-phase flows with unmatched densities, within the framework of a diffuse-interface model, which is called the Abels--Garcke--Grün (AGG) model. More precisely, we adopt energy methods to establish several stability/instability conditions for the perturbed system of the AGG model, which is obtained by slightly perturbing the equilibrium profile $(\mathbf{0},\overline{\varphi})$, where $\overline{\varphi}=\overline{\varphi}(x_3)$ depends solely on the vertical coordinate $x_3$. Notably, for a constant equilibrium profile $\overline{\varphi}=α\in(-1,1)$ satisfying $Ψ''(α)<0$, we establish a threshold for stability and instability of the phase-separation problem.Similar stability results are also established for the perturbed system with $\overline{\varphi}$ satisfying the RT instability condition in the presence of gravity. In particular, if the phase-separation condition fails and $\partial_{x_3}\overline{\varphi}$ is a constant, a larger value of $\partial_{x_3}\overline{\varphi}$, a greater interfacial thickness and a higher surface energy density contribute to inhibiting RT instability.
