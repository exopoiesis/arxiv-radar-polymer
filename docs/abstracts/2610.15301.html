Design for Machinability: Topology Optimization for Subtractive Machining Using Trimmed Neural Fields

Hongrui Chen, Levent Burak Kara

arXiv:2610.15301·cs.GR·Published 2026-10-11

Topology optimization has found significant utility in additive manufacturing, but the resulting geometries are often difficult to realize using subtractive methods. The complex, curved surfaces of optimized designs typically require time-consuming point milling operations. In contrast, geometries composed of developable surfaces are well suited to flank milling, enabling substantial improvements in machining efficiency and surface quality. However, globally minimizing Gaussian curvature leads to overly simplistic designs. We address this challenge with a novel method that represents geometry using three implicit neural fields, optimized to produce machinable components. The resulting designs consist of regions of developable surfaces separated by seamlines where curvature is concentrated. Seamlines are efficiently computed as intersections of the implicit fields, while the geometry emerges through their product. By constraining surfaces to align with the cutter's normal or tangential direction, our approach further accommodates practical constraints of 3-axis machining. We demonstrate concurrent optimization of topology and Gaussian curvature, enabling control over seamline feature scale. Compared to methods that enforce subtractive machinability without considering milling efficiency, our approach consistently reduces machining time while maintaining competitive structural performance.
