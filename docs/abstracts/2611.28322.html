Moment-Based Transient Network Models for Finite-Strain Viscoelasticity

Stephen T. Castonguay, Pratanu Roy, Sylvie Aubry

arXiv:2611.28322·cs.CE·Published 2026-11-20

A viscoelastic framework is developed through a moment reduction of the hereditary integral defining the free energy of transient polymer networks. Expanding the chain energy about the mean history invariant produces a hierarchy of tensorial moments representing the network formation history. For energies depending on the relative first invariant, the leading approximation recovers Lubliner's model, providing a physical interpretation of its second-rank history tensor as an average of the inverse deformation metrics at chain formation. The next approximation introduces a fourth-rank tensor that accounts for variability among formation configurations. These moments admit equivalent rate forms and efficient recurrence relations, while their evolution permits the establishment of sufficient conditions for dissipation. Comparisons with an exact hereditary representation of the three-term Arruda-Boyce energy demonstrate that the first-moment approximation captures the overall response, while retaining the second moment improves accuracy, particularly under complex loading histories. We also incorporate non-exponential relaxation through distributed detachment kinetics described by the Kohlrausch-Williams-Watts (KWW) function. Assuming thermorheological simplicity, calibration to thermally accelerated butyl-rubber relaxation measurements yields excellent agreement across wide ranges of time and temperature. The calibrated continuous rate spectrum is then discretized into a finite Prony series using equal-probability bins. The proposed framework provides a practical representation of nonlinear transient network behavior that separates the approximation of deformation history from the discretization of the continuous rate spectrum.
