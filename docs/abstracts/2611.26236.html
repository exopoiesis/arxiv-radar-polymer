Flux-induced condensation in a radial geometry

Yoav Tsori

arXiv:2611.26236·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-19

Stirring and shear usually suppress phase separation, but transport through a boundary can induce it. We study a binary mixture that is stable everywhere at rest and fills a two-dimensional annulus. The inner boundary is semipermeable and exchanges material with a permeate held at a fixed chemical potential, so that a steady flux of one component crosses the annulus. Although this driven system carries a steady current and produces entropy, within Model B dynamics its steady state is an equilibrium profile in an effective external field that is logarithmic in the distance from the center. Its strength is not imposed from outside but set by the permeate and the permeability of the boundary. Above a threshold driving flux, a condensed annulus forms around the inner boundary. It is bounded by a stationary front whose radius depends exponentially on the ratio of the bulk driving force to the flux. The front is pinned by the effective field and does not coarsen, and the threshold flux decreases only as the inverse logarithm of the system size. The driving flux shifts the phase lines but leaves the critical temperature unchanged. Interfacial tension gives rise to a critical nucleus and to a fold, at which the condensed state ceases to exist, with a threshold that we obtain in closed form. Numerical solutions confirm these predictions and reveal a hysteresis loop bounded by two saddle-node bifurcations. By symmetry, the theory also describes cavitation around a sink.
