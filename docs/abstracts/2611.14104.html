Purely elastic centre-mode instability in channel and pipe flows of a simplified Phan-Thien-Tanner fluid

Calum S. Skene, Jeffrey S. Oishi, Keaton J. Burns, Moritz Linkmann, Geoffrey M. Vasil, Alexander Morozov

arXiv:2611.14104·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-10

Recent work identified spatially localised midplane stress structures as the building blocks of chaotic behaviour in pressure-driven parallel shear flows of viscoelastic fluids. Such coherent structures are thought to originate from a linear centre-mode instability of the laminar state. In the absence of inertia, however, the Oldroyd-B and FENE-P constitutive models predict that this instability is confined to ultra-dilute polymer solutions and very large Weissenberg numbers in channel flow, while no purely elastic instability has been reported in pipe flow. Here, we study numerically the linear stability of channel and pipe flows of a simplified Phan-Thien-Tanner (sPTT) fluid and find a purely elastic centre-mode instability in both geometries across a wide range of polymer concentrations and at much lower Weissenberg numbers. Since the sPTT and FENE-P models have identical steady rheology and laminar profiles, their contrasting predictions indicate that the existence of the centre-mode instability is governed by the transient rheological response of the fluid. Our results suggest that the purely elastic centre-mode instability should be observable under realistic flow conditions. They also offer a means of discriminating between constitutive models.
