Parallel Two-Photon Lithography: From Optical Nonlinearities to Energy Limits, Dose Control and Throughput Benchmarks

Michalis Stavrou, Panagiotis A. Loukakos, David Gray, Maria Farsari

arXiv:2611.29010·physics.optics·Published 2026-11-21

Two-photon lithography (TPL) prints three-dimensional polymer architectures with sub-diffraction features, but writing them voxel by voxel limits its throughput. This review examines how parallelization overcomes this limit with fixed multi-focus arrays, reconfigurable holography using spatial light modulators and digital micromirror devices, hybrid metalens arrays, shaped beams, temporally focused projection and two-step absorption. An energy-deposition framework built on measured nonlinear absorption coefficients shows how the absorption order and coefficients of a resist determine its voxel size, energy per voxel and attainable voxel rate. Applied to parallel writing, the framework quantifies the costs of non-uniform foci, coherent crosstalk between foci, side-lobe losses in shaped beams and weaker axial confinement in projection. Published systems reach peak rates above $10^{8}$ voxels s$^{-1}$ with scanned focus arrays and up to $1.7\times10^{9}$ voxels s$^{-1}$ with grayscale projection under a fine-voxel definition, and parallel features as small as 20-55 nm. As rates rise, however, dose uniformity, proximity effects, heat, stitching and data bandwidth increasingly limit fidelity. A minimum reporting standard that includes the energy per voxel and sustained part-level rates is therefore proposed, together with priorities for the field: shared benchmark parts, predictive dose control and resists designed for parallel writing.
