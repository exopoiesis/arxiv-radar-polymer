Sprouting Fields of Conducting Polymer Dendrites as Evolving Organic Electrochemical Frameworks to Interconnect Electrode Arrays

Antoine Baron, Ewelina Cechosz, Konrad Szaciłowski, Enrique H. Balaguera, Sébastien Pecqueur

arXiv:2611.27480·physics.app-ph·Published 2026-11-20

Balancing high performance with sustainability in microelectronics is challenged by energy-intensive processes, scarce materials and limited recyclability. Inspired by nature, a promising solution relies on the evolution of the foundry model to transient microelectronics, using electrochemistry to grow adaptive circuits on demand and during operation. To this end, this study explores how to harness the morphogenesis of conducting polymer dendrites between stacks of micro-electrode arrays to dynamically interconnect them with voltage pulses. Using propylene carbonate as a solvent, we investigated the dynamics of the electroconvective growth of poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT), revealing strong structural dependencies between the morphology of the electrogenerated polymer and the confinement of the micro-electrodes. As the system evolves from 2D flat patterns to 3D dendritic structures, the capability of the conducting polymer to locally interconnect specific nodes heavily depends on the size and density of the arrays which directly impact the flow distribution during growth. By unraveling how a device geometry enables the growth of dendritic interconnections on integrated micro-electrode arrays, the morphogenesis of conducting polymer dendrites not only addresses the longstanding issue of heterogeneous integration in microelectronics, but also opens the way to a paradigm where electronic devices learn as they grow in our pockets.
