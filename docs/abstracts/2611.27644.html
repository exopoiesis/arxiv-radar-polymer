A nonlinear transient finite element analysis of coupled solvent diffusion and large deformation of magneto-elastic hydrogel balloon

Nadeem Karim Shaikh, Ganesh Tamadapu

arXiv:2611.27644·physics.class-ph·Published 2026-11-20

This study investigates the transient swelling of an initially spherical magnetoelastic hydrogel balloon. The balloon is inflated at constant internal pressure and exposed to the non-uniform magnetic field of one or two current-carrying coils. The gel is modelled as a compressible Ogden network mixed with solvent through Flory--Huggins theory, with an additive elastic--mixing--magnetic decomposition of the free energy. A fully coupled nonlinear finite element formulation uses a staggered solution scheme and a Taylor--Hood-type discretisation. It follows the balloon from its dry inflated state to either swollen equilibrium or burst. The formulation recovers the analytical free-swelling stretch and the delayed-burst regime, and its converged states agree with equilibrium energy minimisation. Symmetry-breaking (pear-shaped) dry configurations do not survive swelling: they rearrange rapidly and then relax by diffusion onto the symmetric branch. Transient membrane stresses can exceed their equilibrium values by nearly an order of magnitude. For coils which are sufficiently close to undeformed balloon, fields beyond a critical intensity close the slow-inflation curve into a loop. Stable swollen equilibria then exist only within a bounded pressure window that narrows as the field increases, and outside of it the balloon bursts. Under two coaxial coils, asymmetry about the equator appears only when the coils differ in current or radius. The magnetic field therefore acts as a control parameter for shape of balloon and permits or prevents stable swelling at a given pressure.
