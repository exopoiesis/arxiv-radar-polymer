Influenza A Virus as a Multivalent Active Particle: Statistical Mechanics of Binding, Motility, and Antiviral Design

Sumati Bhatia, Andreas Herrmann, Thomas Kolbe, Lalan Kumar, Daniel Lauster, Susanne Liese, Bortolo Matteo Mognetti

arXiv:2611.09696·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-06

In this contribution, we review existing multivalent models of Influenza A Virus (IAV) systems to study virion dynamics and design of multivalent entry blockers. We review existing biochemical estimations of the dissociation constants and catalytic activities of the two virion ligands [hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA)], which can be used to develop quantitative theories and experiments. Multivalent models are statistical mechanics frameworks for studying the simultaneous interaction of multiple reversible ligand-receptor bonds. They quantify the effects of both combinatorial entropy terms and single-molecule ligand-receptor affinities. Multivalent frameworks led to the rationale of superselectivity, the ability of probes to sense specific concentrations of conjugated receptors. Multivalent models guided the design of antiviral nanomedicines constituted by polymeric scaffolds conjugated with small molecules such as sialic acid receptors targeting the ligands on the surface of the virion. Importantly, neuraminidase is an enzyme that limits the number of host-guest bonds through receptor desialylation. Like active particles, neuraminidase activity enhances virion motility in the extracellular medium. Modeling the dynamics of Influenza A virions requires developing out-of-equilibrium multivalent models.
