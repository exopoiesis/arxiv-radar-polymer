Study of Randomness in Quantum Random Number Generator using Bayesian Neural Networks

Shriram Pragash M, Aditi Das, Megha Shrivastava, Mohit Mittal, R. Srikanth, Venkat Abhignan

arXiv:2611.19334·quant-ph·Published 2026-11-14

We propose a Bayesian neural network (BNN) framework to quantify the intrinsic (aleatoric) uncertainty in a continuous-variable quantum random number generator (CV-QRNG). This provides a direct data-driven measure of the QRNG's randomness generation. The BNN is trained on raw homodyne voltage time-series data from a fiber-coupled vacuum-fluctuation QRNG (VFQRNG). For each voltage data segment, the network predicts a Gaussian distribution (mean and variance) for the next sample, and the ensemble-averaged predicted variance is used as a diagnostic of entropy-related behavior under assumptions of our quantum source model. Thus, the predicted variance serves as a BNN model-based monitor of the randomness-generation process. We validate that the BNN metric increases with the local-oscillator (LO) power, consistent with theoretical min-entropy (extractable randomness). We also show the metric is sensitive to practical imperfections, coarse-graining (simulating limited analog-to-digital converter (ADC) sampling) or reducing ADC bit depth causes a clear drop in predicted aleatoric uncertainty, consistent with the source min-entropic model. These results establish the BNN-based metric as a complementary, physically motivated diagnostic tool for QRNG characterization. Unlike conventional tests (e.g. autocorrelation) that miss hardware issues like low ADC resolution, our method provides a direct real-time monitor of the VFRNG performance. Therefore, this metric is useful for real-time monitoring of CV-QRNG conditions, including drifts and other imperfections.
