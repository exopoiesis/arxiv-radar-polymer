The Fiscal and Economic Implications of Green Mandates: a Boltzmann Approach to Public Governance

Katerina Savvaidou, Georgia Foudoukidou-Louka, Ho-Ryun Chung, Orestis Loukas

arXiv:2611.27923·cond-mat.stat-mech·Published 2026-11-20

Public authorities increasingly need to translate multifaceted policy objectives into concrete fiscal and investment choices while accounting for their socioeconomic consequences. A credible ex-ante impact assessment should report downstream implications attributable to the historical baseline and specified policy targets, without introducing discretionary assumptions about unknown administrative, socioeconomic or other physical processes. Bringing the large-scale perspective of statistical physics to public governance, we construct such an ex-ante impact assessment. Its scope encompasses policy objectives that condition a finite number of generalized moments of observables in the historical baseline, which comprises a sufficiently large collection of projects or public programmes. As a proof-of-principle application, we consider the green transition in the energy-generation sector using the historical baseline of many infrastructure projects from the World Bank. Our earlier results deriving closed-form expressions for coarse-grained combinatorial probabilities enable a tractable statistical and downstream assessment. We assess the statistical compatibility of the historical baseline with different levels of green-power surplus and demonstrate how the same statistical-physics construction can estimate any downstream metric expressible through arbitrary functions of generalized moments of the baseline observables. In the absence of more detailed knowledge about the processes forming the historical baseline, large-scale universality, whenever many unknown influences on collective phenomena become negligible, unambiguously offers a principled route to a self-contained ex-ante impact assessment.
