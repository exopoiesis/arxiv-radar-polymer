Effects of elasticity on the stability of viscoelastic boundary layers

Shengxin Li, Dehua Wang, Feng Xie, Zhu Zhang

arXiv:2611.20525·math.AP·Published 2026-11-16

The elastic effect on the stability of boundary layers is studied for incompressible elastic fluids in the half-plane. It is known from [33] that if the deformation tensor matrix is nonsingular near the boundary, elasticity can prevent the formation of a boundary layer. In this paper, we prove stability properties when the deformation tensor degenerates. Our results are twofold: (1) when the first row of the deformation tensor has a non-degenerate projection onto a fixed direction, the initial-boundary value problem for the two-dimensional viscoelastic Prandtl boundary layer equations in the half-plane admits local well-posedness in Sobolev spaces, without any monotonicity condition on the velocity profile; (2) when the first row of the deformation tensor vanishes simultaneously at one point, the linearized Prandtl boundary layer system is ill-posed. The first result reveals the stabilizing effect of elasticity against the classical Prandtl instability. The second result shows that elastic effects cannot suppress the Prandtl-type instability associated with a non-degenerate critical point of the velocity profile.
