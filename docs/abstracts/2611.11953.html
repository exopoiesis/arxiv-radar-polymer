Elastic frontogenesis sets the spectra of smooth elastic turbulence

Marco Edoardo Rosti, Andrea Mazzino

arXiv:2611.11953·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-09

Most turbulent flows are sustained by inertia, which transfers energy across scales and produces characteristic power-law spectra. Yet dilute polymer solutions also exhibit turbulence when inertia is negligible: in this state, known as elastic turbulence, the velocity field remains spatially smooth while both the velocity and the polymer conformation exhibit robust power-law spectra. How a smooth flow, lacking a hierarchy of eddies, generates such multiscale structure has remained unexplained. Here we show that the missing mechanism is elastic frontogenesis, whereby the flow spontaneously organizes polymer stress into thin sheets that are stretched within their plane and compressed across their thickness, causing their amplitude to grow as they become thinner. Because the fronts are sheet-like structures, this amplification-thinning law uniquely determines the polymer conformation spectrum, while the Stokes response transfers the same geometric scaling to the velocity field, reconciling broadband spectra with spatial smoothness. Being geometric rather than model-specific, elastic frontogenesis provides an organizing principle for viscoelastic flows and the viscoelastic analogue of vortex stretching in inertial turbulence.
