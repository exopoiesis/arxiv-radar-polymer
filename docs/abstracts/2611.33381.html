Magnetically Guided Sequential Multi-Filler Alignment Enables Anisotropic Heat Pathways in Stretchable Composites for Soft Electronics

Altynay Kaidarova, Viktor Naenen, Isidro Florenciano, Ruben Windey, Xiaoyan Qing, Laurens Snels, Juhyung Park, Tom Van der Donck, Jeroen Soete, David Seveno, Francisco Molina-Lopez

arXiv:2611.33381·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-24

Stretchable, skin-conformal electronics demand thermal interface materials that combine high through-plane heat transport with mechanical softness and lateral electrical insulation. In particle-filled composites, these requirements are typically antagonistic. Heat transport is governed not only by the intrinsic thermal conductivity of the fillers but, more critically, by thermal resistance at the interparticle interface. As the interparticle distance is reduced by increasing the particle content, mechanical reinforcement and in-plane electrical conductivity become unavoidable. The existing particle alignment processing strategies result in inefficient uncoupling of multifunctionality. Here, we present a hierarchical two-step process strategy to induce strong conductivity anisotropy in a soft composite. First, an align-before-blend process forms a scaffold of magnetically aligned nickel (Ni) microparticles in air before polymer infiltration. The absence of the viscous elastomer matrix during the particle alignment facilitates handling large amounts of particles, which assemble into highly ordered and densely packed vertical heat-conduction pathways. Second, a liquid metal (LM)/Ni mixture is infiltrated into the aligned scaffold to bridge interparticle gaps, minimizing interparticle thermal resistance. The composite reaches 7.5 W/(m K) through-plane thermal conductivity with lateral electrical conductivity below $10^{-12}$ S/m and ultrasoft mechanical compliance (approximately 160 kPa), enabling coupling layers in thermoelectric generators and finger-mounted heat-spreading pads for wearable technologies.
