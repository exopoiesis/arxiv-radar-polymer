A free-energy approach for stochastic reaction-drift-diffusion lattice simulations of crowded biological systems

R. M. Acda, A. Parvez, A. T. Apsley, H. Youk, Z. Luthey-Schulten

arXiv:2611.22902·physics.bio-ph·Published 2026-11-17

Molecular size alters spatial distributions, reaction equilibria, and transport in crowded mixtures, but resolving individual particle encounters can make large stochastic simulations computationally costly. We developed a lattice-based framework for simulating reaction-drift-diffusion systems with volume exclusion captured as a free energy functional in each voxel. Changes in this free energy, together with external one-body potentials, modify diffusion and reaction propensities. Our framework accommodates mixtures of particle sizes without resolving each particle's position within a voxel. We implemented our framework in an operator splitting scheme and compared our predictions with off-lattice interacting-particle simulations. Inclusion of the local free energy functional improved spatial occupancies and shifts in reaction equilibria relative to point-particle simulations. With separate approximations for larger spheres and polymers, we simulated crowding-induced slowing and spatial segregation. We then modeled motor proteins' traffic jams on a structured track representing a microtubule. Our results demonstrate how local composition can inform stochastic reaction and transport rates without resolving individual encounters.
