Holographic Drag Force and the Entanglement Membrane: Geometric Reconstruction and Information Spreading

Dmitry S. Ageev

arXiv:2611.13088·hep-th·Published 2026-11-09

A central question in holography is how different boundary observables encode and reconstruct the geometry of the gravitational dual. In this work, we uncover an exact geometric correspondence between two seemingly distinct aspects of strongly coupled dynamics: the dissipative drag force experienced by a moving heavy particle(quark) and the entanglement membrane governing the propagation of quantum information. We show that both constructions retain the same underlying structure of the geometry, leading to an invertible transformation between the finite-velocity drag force and the geometric membrane tension. This correspondence reveals that nonlinear momentum loss encodes detailed information about entanglement dynamics: the leading nonlinear correction determines the butterfly velocity, while higher-order terms successively probe the local geometry of the membrane near the horizon. We further connect this hierarchy to scalar pole-skipping, establishing an all-order relation between nonlinear dissipation and near-horizon spectral data. The shared geometric structure also has implications for quantum information recovery, with the quintic drag coefficient determining the leading dependence of information velocity on initial entanglement. Conversely, the complete information-velocity curve provides a reconstruction of the reduced bulk geometry along the physical interior membrane branch, complementing the exterior reconstruction from drag. We show that stochastic momentum broadening supplies the additional radial information needed to reconstruct the complete exterior metric up to an overall scale. Our results thus reveal how dissipation, entanglement dynamics, and spectral properties provide complementary access to holographic geometry, establishing a unified framework for understanding the geometric information encoded in distinct boundary observables.
