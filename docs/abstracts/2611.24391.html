Structure and Thermal Conductivity of Epoxy-Graphene Oxide Composites from 4.5 to 300 K

A. Jeżowski, A. I. Krivchikov, O. A. Korolyuk, V. V. Sagan, Y. V. Horbatenko, S. V. Cherednychenko, D. Szewczyk

arXiv:2611.24391·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-18

The microscopic mechanisms of heat transport in structurally disordered polymer-carbon composites remain incompletely understood, particularly over the broad temperature range extending from cryogenic to ambient conditions. Here, we investigate the relationship between structural organization and thermal transport in epoxy-graphene oxide (GO) composites with nominal GO contents of 0.25-10 wt.% relative to the resin (0.21-6.1 wt.% of the total composite mass). X-ray diffraction reveals predominantly amorphous scattering without a detectable GO(001) reflection, suggesting substantial suppression of ordered GO stacking within the cured epoxy matrix. Raman spectroscopy confirms the increasing contribution of the carbon phase while revealing no pronounced changes in the characteristic vibrational bands of the epoxy network. Thermal-conductivity measurements (4.5-300 K) demonstrate that GO incorporation modifies the temperature dependence of heat transport rather than producing a simple conductivity enhancement. The experimental data are described by a phenomenological multichannel model comprising a temperature-independent plateau term and two or three effective Arrhenius-type contributions characterized by distinct temperature scales ranging from a few to several hundred kelvin. The low-temperature conductivity plateau observed at lower GO concentrations is no longer resolved in GO-6 and GO-10, indicating a qualitative change in the temperature dependence of heat transport. These results establish a correlation between structural organization and the redistribution of effective thermal-transport contributions in epoxy-GO composites, providing an experimental basis for further microscopic interpretation within the framework of unified vibrational thermal-transport theory.
